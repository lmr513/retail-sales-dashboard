# Retail Sales Decision Dashboard

## 1. Dataset and Business Context

**Dataset:** Sample Superstore

**Source:** Sample Superstore CSV used in this project.

The dataset contains retail order records, including order and shipping dates, sales, profit, quantity, discount, product categories, customer segments, and geographic information. Each row represents an order record. The dataset is a historical snapshot rather than a live feed.

## 2. Decision-Maker and Business Goal

**Target user:** Retail store manager.

The manager needs to understand sales performance, monitor profitability, and identify product categories or regions that may require further investigation.

The dashboard supports recurring business reviews by bringing sales and profit indicators together in one place.

**Decision statement:** If the manager sees declining sales, weak profit, or a product category with substantial sales but poor profitability, they can investigate the relevant period or category and decide whether to review pricing, discount policies, or product performance.

Without the dashboard, the manager would need to filter spreadsheets and prepare separate summaries manually. This takes time and makes it harder to compare the same indicators consistently.

## 3. Why a Dashboard?

The manager needs to revisit sales and profitability questions regularly, not just answer a single question once. A dashboard makes it possible to explore different time periods, regions, categories, and customer segments interactively.

A weekly email or static report could summarize performance, but it would be less convenient for investigating unexpected changes. A dashboard allows the manager to move from an overall result to the relevant subset of records without preparing a new report each time.

## 4. What the Dashboard Shows

### Headline Metrics

- **Total Sales:** Measures the sales value generated by the selected records.
- **Total Profit:** Shows the combined profit or loss for the selected records.
- **Profit Margin:** Shows profit as a percentage of sales, helping distinguish sales volume from profitability.

The dashboard also displays the number of records to provide context for the selected data.

### Supporting Charts and Filters

- **Monthly Sales Trend:** Shows how sales change over time and helps identify periods that may need investigation.
- **Sales and Profit by Category:** Compares product categories to identify differences between sales volume and profitability.
- **Profit by Customer Segment:** Helps the manager compare the profitability of different customer groups.
- **Sales by State:** Highlights geographic differences in sales performance.
- **Discount Analysis:** Helps investigate how different discount levels are associated with profit.
- **Sub-Category Summary:** Provides additional detail when the manager needs to investigate individual product groups.

The date-range, region, category, segment, and state filters allow the manager to focus on relevant records.

### What I Chose Not to Emphasize

1. **Individual order records as a headline view:** Showing every transaction immediately would make the main business indicators harder to identify. Detailed records remain available for investigation when needed.

2. **A large collection of unrelated charts:** Adding every possible breakdown would make the dashboard crowded. The selected views focus on sales trends, profitability, and comparisons that can guide further investigation.

## 5. Data Quality and Potential Misinterpretation

The dataset is historical, so its patterns may not reflect current business conditions. The dashboard displays the dataset's date coverage and should not be interpreted as a live sales monitoring system.

Sales alone do not indicate business success: a category can generate high sales while producing low or negative profit. The manager should therefore review sales and profit together.

Discount levels may be associated with lower profitability, but the relationship alone does not prove that discounts caused the change. Other factors, such as product mix and order characteristics, may also matter.

The dataset may contain unusual transactions or incomplete records. Data preparation handles invalid numeric values and unusable order dates for the main dashboard calculations. Findings should be investigated before making major pricing or product decisions.

## 6. Refresh Frequency and Business Actions

**Proposed production refresh:** Nightly, using the business's order data.

**Review frequency:** Weekly, with an additional review when an unusual result appears.

A nightly refresh would provide sufficiently recent information for routine sales and profitability reviews without requiring continuous real-time processing. The current CSV is a static snapshot, so the deployed dashboard does not automatically receive new transactions.

**Metric to monitor:** Profit margin.

**Proposed action threshold:** If the selected period's profit margin falls below 10%, or falls by at least 5 percentage points compared with the preceding comparable period, the manager should investigate the categories, regions, and discount levels contributing to the change. These are proposed review thresholds, not established company policies, and should be adjusted to the business's actual targets.

Stale data could cause the manager to miss recent losses or continue an ineffective pricing decision. In production, the dashboard should display the latest successful refresh date so users can judge whether the information is current.
